# 1 John 2:28–3:3 — Research Brief

## Project status

**PILOT-001 sermon range:** 1 John 2:28–3:3.

This is a project-level sermon segmentation within a larger disputed literary complex. It is not a claim that all technical outlines recognise 2:28–3:3 as one formal paragraph.

## Governing textual flow

### 2:28 — remain in him because he will appear

The repeated command to remain/abide directly continues 2:27 and therefore should sound as continuity with the preceding sermon rather than as an unrelated restart.

The new incentive is eschatological: Christ's appearing/coming puts present abiding in the horizon of future confidence rather than shame.

**Research support:** YARB-1J-001; KEAT-1J-002; HEIL-1J-002; PAIN-1J-001.

### 2:29 — righteousness reveals family origin

The verse moves from divine righteousness to the person who does righteousness and has been born of God.

Do not construe righteous practice as the cause of divine birth. In the flow of 1 John it functions as family resemblance/evidence consistent with divine parentage.

The first pronoun is genuinely disputed. Yarbrough reads Christ in the first clause and God as the begetter; Painter prefers God throughout; Keating explicitly acknowledges the ambiguity and strongly favours the Father as the begetter.

**Project constraint:** do not make the sermon depend on resolving the first pronoun.

**Research support:** YARB-1J-003; PAIN-1J-003; KEAT-1J-003.

### 3:1 — stop and look at what the Father has given

The imperative "See/Look" functions as a summons to notice something remarkable. The rhetorical force is wonder/attention before exhortation.

The Father's love is expressed in the actual status of believers as God's children. The status is not merely an honorary title.

Painter argues that the "because of this" construction points forward to the following explanation: the world does not recognise God's children because it did not recognise him.

Do not turn "the world does not know us" into a stronger claim of active persecution unless the wider context warrants it.

**Research support:** YARB-1J-004; PAIN-1J-004; KEAT-1J-004.

### 3:2 — already children, not yet fully revealed

The verse holds present identity and future transformation together:

- **now:** we are God's children;
- **not yet:** the full form of what we shall be has not been disclosed;
- **known future:** when Christ appears, believers will be like him because they will see him as he is.

The text warrants real transformation/conformity but also restrains speculation. Do not make detailed claims about the mechanics or full content of the future state from this verse alone.

Yarbrough explicitly notes that the bodily dimension is not stated as fully here as in Pauline parallels. Keating likewise treats the future as more wonderful than present knowledge can specify.

**Research support:** YARB-1J-005; PAIN-1J-005; KEAT-1J-004.

### 3:3 — future hope produces present purification

Verse 3 is not an unrelated moral add-on. It is the present ethical consequence of the hope in verse 2.

Yarbrough describes it as an indicative with prescriptive force: the eschatological hope catalyses present holiness.

The verb for purification can carry ceremonial/ritual associations. Painter notes that possible background but finds no positive indication that baptism is the point. In this context the sources converge on ongoing ethical purification patterned on Christ's purity.

**Research support:** YARB-1J-006; PAIN-1J-006; KEAT-1J-005; HEIL-1J-003.

## Causal / pastoral spine

A compact representation of the passage is:

**Remain in him now because he will appear → those born of God bear the family likeness → look at the Father's love: you are his children now → what you will be is still ahead, but you will be like Christ → therefore Christian hope purifies life in the present.**

This is a representation of the passage's movement, not yet a sermon outline.

## Governing cautions

- Do not let ethical testing swallow the assurance language.
- Do not let assurance erase the ethical entailment.
- Do not make doing righteousness the cause of new birth.
- Do not force the 2:29 pronoun ambiguity.
- Do not over-specify future likeness beyond the text.
- Do not turn 3:3 into self-atonement or ritual cleansing.
- Do not import the full 3:4–10 sinlessness problem into this sermon unless needed to prevent misunderstanding.
